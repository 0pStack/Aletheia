# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: the demo audience (teachers and classmates at the course presentation) who meet
Aletheia for five minutes and must understand, and feel, its idea. Inside the story: clinical
staff (doctors, nurses, clinic admins) who search, read and write journal notes, and patients
who open their own record to see who has looked at it.

## Product Purpose

A healthcare journal where the record of who looked is worth as much as the record itself.
Medical data stays in SQL; every access becomes an event on a chain that no single party
holds. Success: a viewer leaves convinced that the log is honest, because they saw it prove
itself, and remembers that it was beautiful.

## Current Stage

The design direction is settled and the team is happy with it: the desert scene, the fallen
ice block, dark smoked glass, the login lens and dive. The work now is not to rethink any of
it but to make it **uniform, elevated and enhanced**: every screen on the same glass, the same
type and spacing system, the same states, finished to the level of the landing. Proposals
should refine and extend existing choices, not replace them.

## Brand Personality

Calm, crafted, transparent. Quiet confidence, not spectacle; awe earned through restraint.
Emotional goals: trust and calm first, awe at the craft, clarity about who saw what, and
clinical speed that the atmosphere never slows down.

References: igloo.inc (one hero object, heavy atmosphere, very little UI), Apple product
pages (one idea per screen, confident type), Arc / visionOS (smoked glass floating over a
scene), Linear (precise, deliberate dark product UI).

## Anti-references

- Hospital software (Cosmic, TakeCare, Epic): grey forms, blue-and-white, dense tables.
- SaaS templates: hero-metric dashboards, identical card grids, gradient buttons.
- Health-app cute: pastels, mascots, wellness softness.

## Design Principles

1. Evolve, don't reinvent. Existing choices are decisions, not drafts. Make them consistent
   everywhere and raise the finish; don't swap them out.
2. Show the proof, not the machinery. The chain is felt through what it guarantees
   (a log a patient can read, a badge that says Verified), never through blockchain imagery.
3. One hero per screen. Each view has a single thing to look at; everything else steps back.
4. Atmosphere never blocks the task. Craft lives around the clinical path, not in its way.
5. Calm authority. Nothing shouts; hierarchy comes from scale, light and space, not colour
   alarms. Every screen should tell its part of the story in five seconds.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best effort, no formal WCAG target. Keep what exists: reduced-motion fallbacks for the scene
and transitions, a no-WebGL fallback, readable contrast on glass, keyboard access to the
clinical flow. Fix obvious issues as they are found.
